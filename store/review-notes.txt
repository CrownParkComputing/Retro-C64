REVIEW ENVIRONMENT (summary of our earlier reply)

Retro-C64 is a hardware emulator for the Commodore 64 (1982) under Guideline 4.7. No account, login, credentials or files are needed: the app runs immediately on a clean install. First run opens on a welcome screen: tap "I have done this before" to reach the setup console (or "Get started", then "READY." at the end of the primer -- both arrive at the same place). That console has two buttons; tap "Store Compliance", the left-hand one. It switches the emulator to the bundled free Open ROMs and lands on the Compliance page, where "Open Games" takes you to the demo. Tap "Retro-C64 Demo" and an emulated C64 boots and runs it. To use their own software, users import their own Commodore ROM set and files via the Files app (the original Commodore ROMs are Commodore's copyright and are NOT included, which is the only reason the app ever asks for a file).

The app makes no network request of its own: no authentication, payments, analytics, advertising, AI, or third-party data. The single optional network feature is cover artwork, inert unless the user types their own URL into Settings. No regional differences; free everywhere; English (UK) only.

We do not own a physical iOS device: development is verified on the iOS Simulator (iPhone 17 Pro Max and iPad Pro 13-inch (M5), iOS 26.5, Xcode 26.6), and physical-device testing is done by our public TestFlight group.

The Music tab lists well-known SID tunes only as a catalogue of what the player recognises; none is bundled and nothing is downloaded — an entry plays only if the user has imported that .sid themselves.

WHAT CHANGED SINCE THE 4.3(a) REJECTION

The interface is now drawn entirely in the VIC-II's own sixteen colours (Pepto's measurements of the real chip, the values VICE renders with), replacing a palette this app shared with our other titles. The Music tab's absent entries no longer carry a download icon -- nothing is fetched, and the icon said otherwise -- and its heading now counts the tunes actually present on the device rather than always claiming twenty.
